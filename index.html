How to score

6 axes · three anchors each
Impact
1Nice-to-have — doesn't really matter 3Moves the needle a bit 5Game-changer — life or business alters
Momentum
1Standalone task, no follow-on 3Unlocks one or two things 5Catalyzes many downstream wins
Probability
1Long shot, untested 350/50, depends on execution 5Near-certain — you've done this before
Altitude
1Busywork, no real alignment 3Tied to a quarterly goal 5Directly serves a top-1–3 life/work goal
Cost inverse
1Months of work, multiple people 3A week or two of focused time 5An hour or less
Time-sensitivity
1Whenever — no real rush 3Should happen this month 5Today or it's gone

The full ritual

5 minutes · 6 steps
  1. List candidates. Aim for 5–10. Start with the obvious ones, then write the ones you keep avoiding.
  2. Score each on the six axes. Gut-feel is fine. Don't research-paralysis yourself into doing nothing.
  3. Flag one-way doors. If you can't easily undo it, slow down and demand 2× the evidence.
  4. Sort by score. The top of the list is your real agenda — not your inbox, not other people's urgencies.
  5. Commit to the top three. The rest waits. Revisit them next session or next quarter.
  6. One-way doors get extra scrutiny. Consult someone who's been there. Sleep on it for 24 hours.

When IMPACT isn't the right tool

Sometimes just pick
  • Trivial reversible choices — "where should we eat?" Just pick, move on.
  • Pure exploratory or creative work — let yourself wander before you score.
  • When the gut already said yes — don't outsource your intuition to a formula.
  • When you don't have enough information — go gather data first, then come back to score.
  • For life-threatening or emergency situations — act first, debrief after. This tool is for deliberate decisions, not crisis ones.
